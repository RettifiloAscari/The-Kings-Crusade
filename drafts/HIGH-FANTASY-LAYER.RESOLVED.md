Three things asked for in one pass, because they are the same pass:

1. **Finish Norvatch** — resolve the open item on what Norvatch wants, and give it plot
   beats through the back half of the campaign, where the audit found it disappears after
   Module Six.
2. **D&D-ify the world** — peoples, cultures, monsters, spells and loot spread across the
   setting rather than concentrated in the stat block appendix. Trolls, kobolds, orcs and
   their kin placed where they earn their place.
3. **Make the NPC roster racially diverse** — it is currently almost entirely human, and
   the stat blocks say so in print.

The three are one pass because the racial layer decides who the Norvatch factors are, what
Vale's army is made of, and what lives in the drained places. Doing them separately would
mean touching all fifteen documents twice.

---

## 0. The bug that started this

Every stat block in the campaign reads `Medium humanoid (human)`. **The SRD entries they
were built from all read `Medium humanoid (any race)`.** So the human-only cast is not a
design decision anybody made — it is a transcription error, repeated nine times, that
quietly narrowed the world. Fixing the type lines is an SRD-fidelity correction that
happens to do a third of this job for free.

---

## 1. The Peoples — principle first

**Realms are multi-racial polities with a characteristic mix, not one-race nations.** No
elf kingdom, no dwarf kingdom. Every realm below has humans in it and most have most
peoples in it; what differs is proportion, institution, and what the place *does* with the
people it has.

The load-bearing example, and the one that proves the principle: **dwarves in Harrowmark
are wyvern-hunters; dwarves in Norvatch are contract-factors.** Same people, two
institutions, and a Harrowmark dwarf meeting a Norvatch dwarf has less in common with them
than with the orc they hunt beside. State that in the sourcebook explicitly — it is the
cheapest possible inoculation against the world reading as a race-per-nation chart.

### Harrowmark — hard country, worked magic, unimpressed people

Humans in plurality; **dwarves** in the crag holds; **orcs** in the high country for as
many generations as anyone has counted; half-orcs and half-elves unremarked. Greywatch is
substantially dwarven and orcish, and its wyvern-watch is the best of both — dwarves on the
pikes, orcs on the ropes, because that is who is strongest at the end of a line with a
wyvern on the other end of it.

This *serves existing canon* rather than decorating it. Harrowmark's people are "not brave,
exactly — unimpressed." An orc hold that has been killing wyverns for nine centuries and
finds the practice unremarkable is the sharpest possible version of that. And because
Harrowmark's land is mundane while its people are not, its casters are all worked-magic
professionals: a dwarven runesmith, an orc war-priest whose prayers are answered, the
king's human battle-mages.

**Orcs are Harrowmark citizens, not an outside menace.** This matters and is not
negotiable in the proposal: the campaign uses orcs as people on both sides, and the enemy
army is not a race.

### Elduvaine — the Living Realm

**Elves** (wood and high) are the oldest inhabitants, and the Kept Season is effectively an
elven institution: woods planted deliberately into a season and held there, some of them
for three centuries. **Gnomes** are the Archive's clerks and the Standing Light's masons —
gnome-cut stone holds light longest, which is why Caer Ysolde glowed. **Halflings** hold
the river parishes, and the entire etiquette of living politely beside a landscape that
eavesdrops is halfling manners exported to a kingdom. Humans throughout.

And **Elduvaine's magic is populated, not just ambient**: dryads in the Kept Season woods,
sprites and satyrs in the older parishes, things in the rivers that are not fish. A dryad
*is* a Kept Season wood with opinions. This is where the wonder budget goes.

### Oksitan — the marching kingdom

Humans and **dragonborn**, the latter an old landed warrior nobility with river-country
holdings. Proposed because the second king — the crown lost in the water — is almost
certainly Oksitan's, and a dragonborn king in full harness going into a spring river is an
image the campaign can use for the rest of its length. Half-elves common at court.

### Auberitz — the grand duchy that builds things

Humans, **gnomes**, and **halflings**; a mercantile and engineering power rather than a
martial one. **The siege train at Vindana is Auberitz-built and gnome-engineered**, which
gives Module Six's engines a maker and a set of very opinionated artisans to argue with.
Sera Vosk, the Auberitz quartermaster from Module 2A, becomes a rock gnome — a small
change that costs nothing and pays every time she appears.

### Norvatch — the realm that keeps its bargains

**Dwarves** and **tieflings**, with humans throughout. Norvatch is guild-law country: a
bargain is written, witnessed, and binding, and the realm's whole reputation rests on the
fact that it honours the letter of one no matter what the letter turns out to mean. Doria
Kell becomes a tiefling factor.

Dwarven contract law rather than infernal contract law, deliberately — Norvatch is not
sinister and must not read as the devil-realm. It is a market with excellent lawyers.

### Vale's occupation — a paid legion, not a horde

**Hobgoblins** as the professional core, with orcs, humans, goblins and bugbears as
auxiliaries and a stratum of Elduvish collaborator clerks.

This is the proposal's single best fit to existing canon. The occupation "issues permits,"
"collects a grain levy on a published schedule," employs clerks, has competent officers,
and contains no fanatics. That is a *legion*. Hobgoblins are disciplined, literate,
bureaucratic soldiers who file paperwork about atrocities, and they explain the occupation's
established character better than humans ever did. Vale did not raise a mob; **he hired an
army that was already good at this and had been waiting for work.**

Mechanically this costs nothing: every SRD block already in use is `(any race)`, so the
Occupation Guard becomes a hobgoblin legionary with the same numbers.

**Marshal Ossian Drell becomes a hobgoblin** — the professional who surrenders when the
position is genuinely lost, because that is what a professional does. **General Ilyana Voss
becomes an orc**, deliberately: orcs fight for Harrowmark at Greywatch and command for Vale
in the field, and the campaign says nothing about either fact. That is the anti-essentialist
point made once, in the place it costs the most to make.

**Maedoc Vale stays human**, untouched. The open question about him is phrased "whether he
is still human," and changing his race would break that gate's wording.

## 2. Norvatch — the resolved agenda

> **This is the one item in this draft that closes a "Not yet decided" entry.** Everything
> else is new invention in open space; this one changes a gate.

### What Norvatch wants

**Norvatch wants to remain the market for whatever comes out of Elduvaine — as a buyer, not
an owner.** It does not want the kingdom. It wants the exclusive standing right to purchase
what leaves it, guaranteed in writing by whoever holds it when the war ends.

It has held exactly that arrangement with the occupation for three years.

### Why this is the right answer

It is the only proposal that makes the existing canon sharper rather than merely adding to
it:

- Canon says Norvatch "trades with the occupied kingdom" and "has an understanding of some
  kind with the men holding it." This names the understanding. **Norvatch is the fence for
  a drained kingdom.** Vale extracts; Norvatch moves it; the market is what makes the
  draining worth doing at that scale.
- Canon says Norvatch "will deal with either side" and "keeps the bargains it makes." Both
  become load-bearing rather than flavour: Norvatch will genuinely sell to the coalition,
  genuinely honour it, and genuinely go on buying from Vale until the day the coalition
  outbids him.
- Canon says buying Norvatch "costs something the coalition would rather not spend." Now it
  is specific: **Norvatch wants the same thing the Promise already sold to Oksitan and
  Auberitz.** There is not enough Elduvaine to pay everyone, and there never was.
- It gives the party a real lever instead of a flavour NPC: **Norvatch's ledgers are the
  only complete record of what has left Elduvaine in three years.** Which is to say, the
  only way to measure how much of the kingdom is already gone — and therefore how close
  Vale is to finishing. The campaign's clock, purchasable, from someone with no reason to
  lie and every reason to charge.

### What it costs the coalition

Doria Kell will sell the ledgers. Her price is a written guarantee of Norvatch's trading
rights in a liberated Elduvaine — signed by Xavier, who has no authority to sign it, over a
kingdom that is not his, exactly as he had no authority to make the Promise and made it
anyway.

**That is the trap, and it is a good one.** Taking the deal means the coalition has now
promised the same kingdom to three different parties. Refusing it means fighting the rest of
the war without knowing what the clock actually says.

### Where the beats go

| Module | Beat | Weight |
|---|---|---|
| 4 | Doria Kell's offer, deepened. She is not a passing information broker; she is the factor of the house that has been buying Elduvaine by weight for three years, and she says so without embarrassment when asked directly. | Existing scene, rewritten |
| 6 | The supply question already present gains its real shape: some of what is feeding Vindana comes through Norvatch bottoms, legally, under a contract nobody in the coalition can void. | Existing DM note, deepened |
| 8 | **New Optional Content:** the Norvatch counting-house in captured Vindana, its factor still open for business, its ledgers intact and for sale. The relief-valve module gets one cold scene, which it can carry. | New, optional |
| 10 | **New scene beat:** Doria names her price before the finale, so the table walks into Module Eleven already knowing the coalition has sold the same kingdom twice over. | New, folded into an existing scene |
| 11 | Norvatch's bargain joins the weights on the final decision — and lands very differently depending on which ending the table takes. If the crown is set down, Norvatch's contract is with nobody, and Doria says so with real professional regret. | Existing scene, extended |

### What stays open

**Whether Norvatch's arrangement with Vale was ever more than commercial is not answered
here, and should not be.** Doria will say it was only ever trade. She is probably telling
the truth. "Probably" is the correct amount.

## 3. The bestiary — where the monsters go

Placed where the fiction already wanted something and had nothing. All SRD 2014.

| Creature | CR | Where | Why there |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Kobolds** | 1/8 | Ashgate tunnels (2B, optional); Vindana's undercity (6/7) | Trap-layers in a siege is the best use kobolds have. The coalition wants the sewers; something small has already thought about that. |
| **Troll** | 5 | The Held Winter (optional, M5 or M9 region content) | A wood that stopped being spring and started being winter would draw exactly one thing down out of the hills. Regeneration makes fire matter, which a party this far in will enjoy solving. |
| **Winter wolves / worgs** | 3 / 1/2 | The Held Winter | The drained wood has an ecology now that it never had as a spring wood. |
| **Will-o'-wisps** | 2 | The Dead Mile | A mile where every habit died. Something moved in. The most frightening place in Elduvaine deserves a monster that is only lights. |
| **Ogre / ettin** | 2 / 4 | The mountain road (2B) | Ordinary overland hazard, exactly the register 2B already runs in. |
| **Dryads, sprites, satyrs** | 1 / 1/4 / 1/2 | Kept Season woods; older parishes | The wonder budget. A dryad is a Kept Season wood with opinions, and losing her wood to the draining is this campaign's grief in one NPC. |
| **Awakened trees / treant** | 2 / 9 | Deep Kept Season | For a table that wants Elduvaine to fight back once. |
| **Water elemental** | 5 | A Listening Water that has gone badly wrong | Canon says some waters "return speech that nobody in living memory said." This is what that becomes if a DM wants it to have a body. |
| **Wight / ghost** | 3 / 4 | Drained parishes | Sparingly. This is not a horror campaign. |
| **Hobgoblins, orcs, goblins, bugbears** | 1/2 – 1 | The occupation, throughout | As above: the legion. |
| **Mage / Priest / Druid / Knight / Berserker / Spy** | varies | Both sides | Spellcasting NPCs so that magic is visible in the world and not only in the party's hands. |

Nothing here displaces a core scene. Everything lands in Optional Content, in region
colour, or as a swap a DM can make deliberately.

## 4. Spells and wonders — the loot layer

Loot is currently, almost everywhere, "masterwork but not magical." That was a defensible
early choice and it has stopped being one: the party starts at 5th level and ends against a
CR 12 archmage.

### Elduvish wonders (new, canon-derived)

The best magic items available here are the four habits, made portable. These are inventions
but they invent nothing new — each is an existing habit in a container:

- **A standing-stone lamp.** Gnome-cut Elduvish stone that holds light poured into it.
  Charge it in daylight, and it gives light for hours. Ordinary in Caer Ysolde before the
  fall; extraordinary anywhere else, and increasingly rare inside Elduvaine now.
- **A flask of Listening Water.** Holds what was said at its mouth and gives it back once,
  in the speaker's voice. An investigation tool, a courier, and a way to make a dying NPC's
  last words a physical object the party carries.
- **A road-token.** Cut from a waystone on a Willing Road. What it measures is still nobody's
  business — the token does not explain itself either, which keeps the deliberately-open
  question shut.
- **Kept Season seeds.** Plant them and a small stand holds the season they were sown in.
  Mostly wonder; occasionally, deliberately, a tactical problem.

### SRD items, tiered up the campaign

Potions of healing and a driftglobe early; a +1 weapon, cloak or boots of elvenkind, goggles
of night, a bag of holding through the middle; a wand, a ring of protection, or one uncommon
item of the DM's choice around Vindana, where Module Seven already flags "the first
genuinely significant material reward of the campaign."

## 5. What this is, honestly

**Natural extension of approved canon:**

- Fixing `(human)` to `(any race)` — a straight SRD correction.
- The occupation as a disciplined paid legion — canon already describes a bureaucracy with
  permits, levies, competent officers and no fanatics. Naming it hobgoblin explains what was
  already written.
- Elduvish fey — canon already says the magic is resident and the land "notices."
- The Elduvish wonders — each is a habit already in canon, put in a container.
- Magic loot at level — a table-specifications consequence, not a setting change.

**Genuine new invention, needing sign-off:**

- **Norvatch's agenda** (closes a *Not yet decided* item).
- **The race-to-realm mix for all five realms** (structural worldbuilding).
- **Dragonborn nobility in Oksitan**, which brushes against the still-open question of the
  second king's realm without answering it.
- **Voss as an orc and Drell as a hobgoblin** (changes established NPCs).
- **Sera Vosk as a rock gnome** (changes an established NPC).

**Deliberately still not answered:**

- What Oksitan and Auberitz *want*. Peoples, not agendas — the gate stays shut.
- The second king's realm. Dragonborn nobility makes Oksitan more vivid; it does not name
  the crown.
- The royal family's names. The Ward, the Magistrate and the Regent gain *peoples* in this
  proposal, not names.
- Whether Vale is still human.
- What the Willing Road measures — the road-token deliberately does not explain itself.
- What the deepest vaults contain.
